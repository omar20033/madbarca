Real Madrid became the first football club in history to pass €1 billion in single-season revenue in 2023/24, driven largely by the renovated Bernabéu. Barcelona's 2025 figure was boosted by one-off income tied to their own stadium rebuild, including seat-licence sales as Camp Nou reopens in phases.

The Stadium Race

Both clubs are mid-renovation, and both projects are among the most expensive in sports history.

Barcelona returned to Camp Nou for competitive matches on 22 November 2025 after more than two years playing home games at the Estadi Olímpic Lluís Companys. Full completion of the 105,000-seat rebuild is targeted for 2026/27, which would make it the largest club football stadium in Europe by a wide margin over the Bernabéu.

What This Doesn't Capture

Revenue and stadium size don't map neatly onto results — Barcelona spent several seasons in serious financial difficulty in the early 2020s while still winning La Liga titles, and Real Madrid's on-pitch dominance long predates its current stadium. Both clubs remain members-owned (socios), unlike most of their major European rivals, which shapes how each reinvests matchday and commercial income back into the first team.
