1899 — Present

Two Clubs, One Rivalry

Founded three years and 500 kilometres apart, Real Madrid and Barcelona grew into the two most successful clubs in Spanish football — and, for much of the 20th century, symbols of opposing ideas of Spain itself.

Real Madrid CF

  • Founded1902
  • HomeSantiago Bernabéu
  • NicknameLos Blancos
  • ColoursAll white

One of only three clubs never relegated from La Liga's top flight. Built its early global reputation on five straight European Cups from 1956–1960, then again through the "Galácticos" era and a run of Champions League wins in the 2010s and 2020s.

FC Barcelona

  • Founded1899
  • HomeSpotify Camp Nou
  • NicknameBlaugrana / Culers
  • ColoursBlue and garnet

Founded by a small group including Swiss businessman Joan Gamper. Also never relegated from the top division. Defined by Total Football under Johan Cruyff, the Guardiola treble-winners of 2009, and the Messi era that produced a record haul of Ballons d'Or.

Timeline

Key moments in the two clubs' history and their rivalry.

1899

FC Barcelona Founded

Founded on 29 November 1899, largely by Swiss, English and Catalan players, under Joan Gamper.

1902

Real Madrid Founded

Founded as Madrid Football Club; granted the "Real" (Royal) title by King Alfonso XIII in 1920.

1902

First El Clásico

The clubs first met in the 1902 Copa de la Coronación, the earliest recorded edition of the fixture.

1956–1960

Five Straight European Cups

Real Madrid won the first five editions of the European Cup, led by Alfredo Di Stéfano and Ferenc Puskás.

1988–1996

Cruyff's Dream Team

Johan Cruyff's Barcelona won four consecutive league titles and the club's first European Cup in 1992.

2008–2009

Guardiola's Sextuple

Barcelona became the first European club to win six trophies in a calendar year, sweeping every competition entered.

2014–2018

Four Champions Leagues in Five Years

Real Madrid won four European titles in five seasons, including three in a row from 2016 to 2018.

2025–2026

Back-to-Back Champions

Barcelona retained the La Liga title with a 2–0 win directly over Real Madrid, their 29th league championship.

More Than A Game

The rivalry has always carried weight beyond the pitch. Real Madrid, based in Spain's capital, was long associated with the Spanish establishment; Barcelona, representing Catalonia, became a vehicle for regional identity, especially during the Franco era when Catalan language and institutions were suppressed. That backdrop is part of why El Clásico still draws a global television audience unmatched by almost any other club fixture — two histories, two identities, and a scoreline that, after 264 meetings, still can't separate them.
